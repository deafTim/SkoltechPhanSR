\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Single-image super-resolution (SR) reconstructs a high-resolution image from a
single low-resolution observation. Deep learning has transformed the field:
early convolutional models such as SRCNN and EDSR~\cite{dong2014srcnn,lim2017edsr}
established end-to-end learned priors, while residual attention and transformer
architectures~\cite{zhang2018rcan,liang2021swinir,conde2022swin2sr} further
improved fidelity and texture realism. As a result, SR is now a practical
component in mobile imaging, remote sensing, scientific visualization, and
content-delivery pipelines.

In practice, however, the SR image is rarely the final product. After restoration
it is usually compressed again before storage or transmission. This creates a
mismatch: the SR model maximises visual detail, while the overall system cares
about how well the result survives compression. The fine textures that make
modern SR look impressive---especially under perceptual
objectives~\cite{ledig2017srgan,blau2018perception}---are often the same
features that raise image entropy and increase bits per pixel (bpp). Even under
a strong neural codec~\cite{cheng2020learned}, a super-resolved DIV2K
crop~\cite{Timofte_2018_CVPR_Workshops} can still require a non-trivial bitrate
because the SR network invents high-frequency content that the codec must encode.

\paragraph{Research question.}
Given a pretrained SR model and a fixed neural codec, can we adapt the SR output
so that the compressed reconstruction becomes cheaper to encode while preserving
the quality of the original SR result?

\paragraph{Approach.}
We study compression-aware \emph{per-image} fine-tuning. For each image, the
pretrained SR output is treated as the fidelity reference; the codec remains
frozen; and only the SR parameters are updated with an ADMM-inspired
scheme~\cite{boyd2011admm} that penalises bitrate and controls fidelity via
PSNR terms and a projection onto a quality ball around the reference. Unlike
dataset-level joint training of SR and compression, the target is compressibility
of an \emph{existing} SR backbone at deployment time. The regime is offline or
high-value adaptation (archival preparation, remote-sensing products, one-shot
restoration that is stored many times), not real-time processing of every frame.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A constrained formulation that minimises codec bitrate subject to PSNR
    constraints relative to the pretrained SR reference (not ground-truth HR as
    the optimisation target).
    \item An ADMM-inspired per-image optimisation with an auxiliary image
    variable and projection onto a PSNR ball.
    \item Instantiation on NinaSR~\cite{gouvine2021torchsr} and
    Swin2SR~\cite{conde2022swin2sr} (full fine-tuning and
    LoRA~\cite{hu2021lora}), with evaluation under a shared operating-point rule
    that couples bitrate minimisation with an explicit PSNR guarantee.
\end{enumerate}
